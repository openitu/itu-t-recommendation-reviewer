# Rules for ITU-T | ISO/IEC common texts (Presentation Rules, 09/2014)

For joint ITU-T | ISO/IEC documents these rules **override** the Author's guide. Where the Presentation Rules are silent, editors may use either the Author's guide or ISO/IEC Directives Part 2 — so only flag deviations from rules explicitly stated here.

## Key deviations from the Author's guide (Annex E of the Presentation Rules)

1. **No appendices.** Non-integral material is presented as **annexes designated "(This annex does not form an integral part of this Recommendation | International Standard.)"**, lettered sequentially A, B, C… continuing after the integral annexes. Finding an "Appendix I" in a common text = critical.
2. Integral annexes carry: "(This annex forms an integral part of this Recommendation | International Standard.)" — centred.
3. Modified boilerplate in the normative references clause (see below).
4. Single-column layout.
5. Notes follow a mixed ITU-T/ISO convention (see Notes below).

## Self-reference conventions (§1, §5.4)

- In Scope and in the boilerplates of clauses 2 and 3: the text calls itself "**this Recommendation | International Standard**".
- Elsewhere: a capitalized descriptive term ("this Specification", "this Framework", …) declared for the document.
- When using "Recommendation | International Standard", do NOT put "ITU-T" before/after "Recommendation".

## Structure (§6, Table 1)

Preliminary: cover, Contents (optional), **Foreword (mandatory)**, Introduction (optional) — may differ between organizations; Roman-numbered pages.
Core: Title / Scope 1 / **Normative references** 2 / Definitions 3 / Abbreviations 4 / Conventions 5 / text 6 onwards / integral annexes / non-integral annexes / Index (optional, last).

- Scope starts immediately after the title on page 1; **Scope shall not contain requirements**; Introduction shall not contain requirements.
- Title: elements from general to particular, max ~3 elements, introductory element "**Information technology**"; "Part n" does not appear in titles of multi-part standards.

## Clause 2 — Normative references

- Heading is "Normative references" (not "References").
- Boilerplate (differs from pure ITU-T!):

> "The following Recommendations and International Standards contain provisions which, through reference in this text, constitute provisions of this Recommendation | International Standard. At the time of publication, the editions indicated were valid. All Recommendations and Standards are subject to revision, and parties to agreements based on this Recommendation | International Standard are encouraged to investigate the possibility of applying the most recent edition of the Recommendations and Standards listed below. Members of IEC and ISO maintain registers of currently valid International Standards. The Telecommunication Standardization Bureau of the ITU maintains a list of currently valid ITU-T Recommendations."

- Subdivided: 2.1 Identical Recommendations | International Standards; 2.2 Paired Recommendations | International Standards equivalent in technical content; 2.3 Additional references.
- Identical texts referenced with both designations: "Recommendation ITU-T X.613 (1992) | ISO/IEC 10588:1993, <title>." Paired texts listed as two consecutive entries (ITU-T first, then ISO/IEC).
- Referencing an identical Rec | IS from the text uses dual notation with "Rec.": "see Rec. ITU-T X.882 | ISO/IEC 13712-3 and Amd.1."
- Informative sources → non-integral annex titled "Bibliography", placed as the last non-integral annex.

## Clause 3 — Definitions
Opens with: "For the purposes of this Recommendation | International Standard, the following definitions apply". May contain subdivisions listing terms defined in other Recs/ISs.

## Clause 4 — Abbreviations
Opens with: "For the purposes of this Recommendation | International Standard, the following abbreviations apply".

## Subdivisions, references to them (§5.5, §6.10.1–6.10.2)
- "clause 3" for top level; "3.1" or "clause 3.1" acceptable below; avoid "subclause" as a designator.
- Don't create 1.1 unless 1.2 exists.

## Figures, tables, equations (§6.10.4–6.10.6)
- Same core rules as ITU-T (explicit in-text reference, Arabic numbering from 1, figure title below / table title above, centred, "Figure x – " / "Table x – " prefix, uppercase F/T in references).
- Long tables: "(continued)" at bottom of first page, "Table x (continued)" on intermediate pages, "Table x (concluded)" on last.
- Wide tables: sub-tables with row index numbers 1a/1b etc., reconstruction layout rule.
- Equations use style "Equation", numbered (1), (2)… consecutively; (6-1) style exceptional for large publications.

## Notes (§6.10.7, Annex A)
- Shall NOT contain requirements (main-text notes).
- Single note in a subdivision: "NOTE – "; several in the same subdivision: "NOTE 1 – ", "NOTE 2 – "… (alternatively continuous numbering throughout the publication).
- Note indented from the main-text margin.
- Notes to tables/figures: inside table frame / immediately above figure title; sequence always starts "NOTE 1 – "; MAY contain requirements.

## Use of words (§6.10.8)
- "shall" = mandatory; "may" = optional; negative of "may" is "**need not**"; "**may not**" shall be avoided → search the text for "may not" and flag every instance.

## Amendments & technical corrigenda (Annex C)
- Called "Technical Corrigendum" (not just corrigendum) in common texts.
- Page 1 title area: base title + "Amendment n" + amendment title / + "Technical Corrigendum n" (no corrigendum title).
- Headers/footers use "Amd." / "Cor.".
- Bold numbered headings give the change location by clause number (never page number); instructions to the reader in *italics*; inserted text in normal base-text style.

## Referencing ITU-T Recommendations (Annex B)
- Normative list: "Recommendation ITU-T X.92 (1988), <title>." — CCITT-era texts also cited as "Recommendation ITU-T".
- In-text: abbreviation "Rec.": "Rec. ITU-T X.200 | ISO/IEC 7498-1".
